01 — The opportunity

People arrive for the drama, then hit a wall.

The audience grew 64% in seven years, and only 16% of new fans credit Drive to Survive — 39% came in through friends and family. They arrive through people and stories, not lap charts. Then the sport hands them tyre compounds, delta times, undercuts and DRS detection points, and expects them to keep up. That drop-off between "I love this" and "I understand this" is the whole opportunity.

What exists today

Official timing apps built for people who already know the sport. Fantasy leagues for the committed. YouTube explainers that are passive and never tied to a race you are watching.

What is missing

A live, visual, replayable race you can interrogate. Something that answers "why did he pit just now?" at the moment you think it, not in a 12-minute video afterwards.

Why we can do it

Our simulation is deterministic and seeded. That means we can rewind it, annotate it, and re-run it with one decision changed. A broadcast can never do that. That is our unfair advantage.

The specific confusions to solve first

Research consistently names the same culprits: tyre strategy (six compounds, degradation, why a slower tyre can win a race) and DRS (the one-second rule, detection points, why it is not a "pass button"). Add the undercut, dirty air, and the difference between pace and position. Every one of these is something our simulation already models — so we can show it rather than describe it.

02 — Who we are building for

Three people, one product.

Primary

The Curious Newcomer

Watched a race with a friend. Enjoyed it. Cannot tell you what a pit window is. Wants to not feel stupid. Needs: plain language, no jargon without a tooltip, a reason to care about a specific driver.

Secondary

The Returning Casual

Watches maybe five races a season. Knows the names, not the strategy. Needs: fast catch-up, "what did I miss", and the ability to poke at a scenario without a 90-minute commitment.

Tertiary

The Strategy Nerd

Already knows everything. Here for the sandbox. Needs: determinism, seeds, the what-if engine, and shareable proof that their strategy call was right.

The nerd is not the target, but the nerd is the distribution. They make the what-if clips the newcomer sees. Build for the newcomer, arm the nerd.

03 — Information architecture

Structure

Borrowed from the console-game pattern in your reference: a persistent top nav, a mosaic dashboard home, and dense detail screens behind each entry. Familiar to gamers, and it scales to more circuits without a redesign.

Landing→Circuits→Circuit detail→ Race simulation (built)→ Race debrief→What-if re-run→Share

Home

Mosaic dashboard. Featured circuit, continue-where-you-left-off, a "learn one thing" card, latest what-if from the community.

Circuits

Grid of tracks. Shanghai live; the rest are honest placeholders marked as coming.

Garage

The seven cars we hold real models for. Rotatable 3D viewer, spec panel, plain-English "what makes this car different".

Drivers

All fourteen. Number, team, a personality hook, and their simulated ratings so the sim is transparent rather than a black box.

05 — Design system

Consistent with what we already built

The simulation already ships Formula1 Display, Formula1 Wide and MonoSpec, plus a dark charcoal palette and a lime signal colour. The landing experience reuses all of it, so the transition from browsing into the race feels like one product.

Type

Three roles, no more.

Formula1 Wide
Brand lockups and hero headings only — it is very wide and overflows tight slots.
Formula1 Display — headings and UI
MonoSpec 1234567890 — all timing
Tabular figures so digits never jitter as they tick.

Colour

Team colours are data, never decoration — each team's hex comes straight from the grid definition.

Lime is reserved for "you can act here". Red is reserved for race-critical and destructive actions. Nothing else competes.

Card mosaic

Console-dashboard grid from your reference. Scales to more circuits without redesign.

Button legend

The persistent bottom hint bar. Cheap, and it makes the whole thing feel like a game rather than a website.

Jargon underline

Every sport term is a dashed-underline tooltip. One pattern, applied everywhere.

Honest placeholders

Unbuilt circuits say so. We never fake content we do not have.

06 — Stickiness & distribution

What makes someone come back, and what makes them post

A landing page alone does not retain anyone. These are ranked by how much they lean on the thing we uniquely have — a deterministic, rewindable race.

Highest leverage

1 · Rewrite the race

Change one decision — pit two laps later, start on the hard tyre — and re-run. Because the sim is seeded and deterministic, the counterfactual is real, not hand-waved. "I made Alonso win at Shanghai" is a genuinely shareable artefact, and no broadcast can produce it.

Retention

2 · Explain Mode

The reason a newcomer stays. Every event annotated with a plain-language "why". Toggleable, so the nerd is never patronised. This is the feature the product is actually about.

Habit

3 · Predict the podium

Before the sim runs, call the top three. Get scored. A weekly streak gives a reason to return that does not depend on the real race calendar — we can run a fresh seed any day.

Distribution

4 · 30-second race recap card

Auto-generated vertical summary: winner, the decisive moment, one stat. Built for the format where F1 video views grew 54% year on year. Ship the share card and the loop closes itself.

Onboarding

5 · Find my driver

Six questions to a driver you care about. Most people pick a driver before a team, and caring about someone is what converts a visit into a habit.

Depth

6 · Corner-by-corner annotations

Tap any corner for why it is hard and where passes happen. Turns a track map from decoration into the reason the racing makes sense.

The honest risk

Everything above is downstream of one thing: the simulation has to produce races worth explaining. If the racing is not dramatic, no amount of annotation saves it. Before building any of this, I would want to watch ten generated races end-to-end and confirm they are actually interesting — overtakes in plausible places, strategies that vary by seed, finishes that are not foregone. That is a cheap check and it de-risks the entire roadmap.

07 — Sequencing

What I would build, in order

PhaseScopeDepends on
P0 The shell. Landing, circuits browser, circuit detail, garage, drivers, and routing into the existing simulation. All static data we already hold. No new simulation work. Nothing — can start now
P1 Explain Mode + glossary. Annotate the existing event feed with plain-language reasons; dashed-underline tooltips everywhere. The core value proposition. Event feed (exists)
P2 Rewrite the race. Rewind to a decision point, change one variable, re-run from the same seed, diff the outcome. Plus the share card. Deterministic engine (exists)
P3 Habit loop. Predict-the-podium, streaks, "find my driver", daily seed. Accounts / local storage
P4 More circuits. Second track proves the pipeline generalises. Our track generator already derives everything from the model, so a new circuit is mostly an asset problem. Licensed track models

P0 is roughly a week of focused work and unblocks review of everything else. P1 is where the product stops being a tech demo.

08 — Before I build

Decisions I need from you

1 · Name. "APEX" is a placeholder I used so the mockups read as a product. Do you want a real name now, or keep it neutral?

2 · Real drivers and teams, or fictional? The mockups use real 2026 names because the simulation does. That is fine for a private prototype, but names, liveries and likenesses are licensed IP and this is already flagged in our credits as unverified. If this ever goes public we should decide now: license it, or go fictional-but-plausible. It is much cheaper to decide before we build a driver-led onboarding flow around real people.

3 · Accounts? Predictions and streaks need persistence. Local storage only (no signup friction, no cross-device) or real accounts?

4 · Which single feature after P0? My strong recommendation is Explain Mode — it is the reason to exist. But if virality is the priority, Rewrite the Race is the more shareable one.

My recommendation

Build P0 as a static shell over what we already have, then P1 Explain Mode. Resist adding circuits until one track is genuinely great — the second track multiplies asset cost without proving anything new. And settle the licensing question before we build identity features around real drivers.
